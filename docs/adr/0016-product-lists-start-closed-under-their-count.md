# Product lists start closed under their count

Acetaminophen is in 55 Alternatives and 348 Combination Products, and a page that listed
them open put 403 rows of product names under four sections of labelling. Nothing in the
rows was wrong. The page was unreadable, and the Safety Sections a reader came for were pushed out
of sight.

So both lists start closed. Each is headed by its true count ("348 products that combine
acetaminophen with another medication"), and a reader who wants the list opens it. Warfarin's
10 Alternatives get the same treatment as acetaminophen's 348 Combination Products: there is
no size threshold, and so no second behaviour.

Three other rules were considered:

- **Cap and count**, showing the first N and then "and 293 more". The cut is arbitrary, and
  the reader cannot tell which products were dropped.
- **Group by dosage form**, as in "Oral tablet: 12 strengths". This puts a dosage form above a
  strength, which ADR-0013 deliberately does not.
- **Drop the Combination Products above some size.** ADR-0005 wants them named, and naming
  fewer of them is how a reader comes to believe the list is complete.

## Consequences

A closed list is still the whole list, and its count is stated up front, so the reader learns
how many products there are without opening it. Alternatives and Combination Products share
one affordance and both start closed, so neither one is easier to see than the other
(ADR-0005).

The lists are still grouped by composition, exactly as ADR-0013 left them. The disclosure does
not replace that grouping, and no row spans two strengths.

A reader who wants the products has to open them, which costs one click on a page where
they used to be visible. That is the price of keeping the labelling on screen.
